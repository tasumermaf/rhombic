\begin{abstract}
Claims read from adapter weights are usually exploratory: representations
chosen after seeing the data, baselines tuned post hoc, headline numbers
never re-derived. We report the complementary experiment---an epistemic
machine, and what it certified. A bank of 480 LoRA adapters (2 model
families $\times$ 6 tasks $\times$ 40 seeds; Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and
Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) was trained under hypotheses locked before the bank
existed; every open analytical choice was pinned in a dated ruling before
the analysis that consumed it ran; a completeness interlock refused every
real-bank statistic until 480/480; each analysis ran exactly once; every
headline was independently re-derived by a separate Director instance; and
the full paper survived a 117-finding adversarial audit. The machinery
certified: raw weights fail a pre-registered task-identifiability lock
(leave-one-out 0.0792 / 0.1375 vs.\ chance 0.1667) while
$\mathrm{GL}(r)$-canonical and vocab-signature representations reach
1.0000; our own no-transfer prediction was refuted by its own triviality
control (shift-controlled transfer 0.7375--0.7833); only the identity
backbone is load-bearing ($+2.8086$ / $+3.8365$ nats when destroyed,
under $+0.001$ otherwise); weight-only features predict midpoint-merge
conflict at group-aware AUC 0.995 / 0.962; and a pilot $r = 0.888$ shrinks
honestly to 0.300. Two of five outcomes went against us---the credibility
of the other three.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A trained LoRA adapter is a small stack of low-rank matrices. Adapters now
accumulate by the thousands---shared on hubs, merged into products, composed
by people who never saw the training run. This paper asks what the weights
alone reveal, post hoc: given only the adapter tensors---no training data,
no logs, no access to the training process---what can be read out, and what
does reading it out require?

The technical obstacle is not information but parameterization. A LoRA
update factorizes as $BA$, and for any invertible $r \times r$ matrix $M$
the reparameterization $(B, A) \rightarrow (BM, M^{-1}A)$ leaves the
effective update unchanged---the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$ gauge. Two adapters
trained on the same task can sit far apart in raw parameter space while
encoding the same update, and adapters from different model families can
sit apart for reasons unrelated to what they learned. Raw flattened weights
are the unexamined default of adapter analysis; this paper measures what
the default costs.

The epistemic obstacle is the one this condensation leads with. Weight-space
readout results are cheap to produce and expensive to trust: the analyst
usually sees the data before choosing the representation, the baseline, the
label rule, and the cross-validation scheme, and the headline is rarely
re-derived by anyone else. We therefore built the study as a measurement
instrument first and a results generator second. Hypotheses were locked in
an experiment card before the adapter bank existed. Every analytical choice
the card left open was pinned in a dated ruling by an independent Director
instance---all but one before the bank completed; the exception, the D3
pair design, was declared minutes after completion, before any pairs,
merges, or labels existed, and ratified on review. A completeness interlock
refused every real-bank statistic until all 480 runs were complete, and it
held. Each of the five locked weight-only analyses then ran against the
real bank exactly once. Every headline number was independently re-derived
by the Director from per-item data, and the released paper was then pushed
through a seven-round adversarial audit that confirmed 117 findings, every
one dispositioned. Two of the five pre-registered outcomes went against
us. A pipeline that can only confirm is not a measurement instrument; the
refutation and the shrink below are what give the three ceiling results
their evidentiary standing.

What the machinery certified, in delivery order: (1)~raw adapter weights
do not reveal their training task, while gauge-canonical and
vocab-signature representations identify all six tasks perfectly---the
information is in the weights; the gauge makes it illegible;
(2)~our pre-registered no-transfer prediction was refuted, specifically
by the triviality control added in review, which exposed raw
transfer-at-chance as a covariate-shift artifact; (3)~the identity
backbone is the sole load-bearing structure for in-distribution loss;
(4)~two adapters' weights alone predict whether their midpoint merge
degrades, with a CI-separated margin over a distance-only
baseline---claimed only in the weight-only, post-hoc, no-training-access
regime; and (5)~a pilot bridge-deviation $\leftrightarrow$
generalization-gap correlation shrinks at bank scale to a real, modest,
within-task-heterogeneous association. Section~\ref{sec:results} carries
the certified numbers.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i)~The bank artifact, with a per-item
verification bundle from which every headline was independently
re-derived. (ii)~The discipline---locked hypotheses, Director-pinned
choices, a completeness interlock that held, dated amendments in place of
silent revisions, maker--grader separation, and a 117-finding adversarial
audit---offered as a reusable template for weight-space empirical work.
(iii)~Five certified results: the canonicalization ceiling (H1), the
refutation-by-control (H2), the backbone result (D2), the post-hoc
merge-conflict predictor (D3), and the honest shrink (D-aux).

\section{The instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}

\subsection{The adapter bank}
\label{sec:bank}

The artifact under study is a bank of 480 LoRA adapters: 2 model families
$\times$ 6 tasks $\times$ 40 seeds. The families are Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct
and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct; the tasks are alpaca, code, math, xsum, squad,
and agnews. Every run trains a rank-24 adapter ($\alpha = 16.0$) on the
q/k/v/o projections, with a 6-channel identity-initialized bridge per
wrapped module: the effective update is $\Delta W = B\,(M \otimes I_4)\,A$,
% SOURCE: C:/falco/rhombic/paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 336-344
% (bridge mechanics; 36 learnable entries of the 6x6 mixing matrix M).
whose 36 learnable entries are the positions the D2 permutation cells
operate on; at identity initialization the bridge is exactly absent
(standard LoRA). Training is AdamW (lr 0.0002, weight decay 0.01, clip
1.0) with linear warmup (100 steps) and cosine decay to a 0.1 floor, for
2000 optimizer steps at effective batch 16 (micro-batch 4 $\times$
accumulation 4) = 32{,}000 sequences of length 512, from a per-task pool
capped at 40{,}000 examples; validation is a fixed 500-example split per
task cut with the locked seed 777---the same split for every analysis that
touches val loss. Precision is bfloat16 base / float32 adapters. Adapter
module counts are discovered from each adapter rather than hardcoded: 112
modules per run for Qwen, 64 for Llama. One dated amendment (A1) changed
the batch geometry mid-campaign after verifying bit-equivalence up to
float summation order; all 480 runs were re-executed under the new
geometry for cohort uniformity, and every config records its cohort tag.

The campaign ran on a single local GPU from July 3 21:52Z to July 20
16:21Z---roughly 17 days including the A1 restart---and the manifest
closed at 480/480 COMPLETE on 2026-07-20T16:21:19Z. The outage ledger
contains exactly four failures, all HF Hub 504 errors on July 16 (run
indices 329--332), all retried to COMPLETE. Measured per-run rates
[measured]: llama3.2-1b mean 30.56 min/run ($n = 240$), qwen2.5-1.5b mean
51.72 min/run ($n = 240$), blended mean 41.14 min/run ($n = 480$).

Chance accuracy for the 6-task classification problems throughout is

\subsection{Pre-registration with teeth}
\label{sec:prereg}

\paragraph{The locked experiment card.} The hypotheses (H1, H2, H3, the D3
prediction target, the D-aux re-verification) were locked in the experiment
card of 2026-07-03, before the bank existed; the card's standing
instruction is that hypotheses are locked and the deliverable fills result
tables only. One provenance gap is reported rather than papered over: the
card was maintained outside version control during the campaign and enters
the repository only with the parent paper, its date corroborated by the
2026-07-06 pipeline documentation that cites it.

\paragraph{Director-pinned decisions.} The Director is a separate AI
reviewer instance with no shared context with the authors and no role in
authoring the analyses; ``pinned'' means committed in a dated ruling before
the consuming analysis ran. The pins that carry the results below: the H2
decision rule (one-sided exact-binomial $\alpha = 0.01$ and a $\ge 15$
percentage-point within-minus-cross margin, required in both transfer
directions, evaluated on the shift-controlled representation with raw
reported as descriptive); the D2 structure reference and the ruling that
the magnitude/topology decomposition cells run unconditionally, because
gating them on whether a contradiction materializes would be a post-hoc
forking point; and the D3 label rule (a fixed 5\% relative-degradation
threshold per endpoint, with a degenerate floor at 10\% class balance
below which the analysis falls back to a pre-declared median split). A
later dated amendment (A3) added the vocab-signature D1 arm, with the
Director independently verifying the condition encodings.

\paragraph{The completeness interlock.} The entire analysis layer was built
and validated against synthetic fixtures only, before the bank finished.
Every CLI that can touch the real bank calls a single gate,
\texttt{require\_complete\_bank()}, before reading a single adapter; the
gate passes only when the manifest---the source of truth, written by the
campaign runner---lists exactly 480 runs, every one COMPLETE. An
\texttt{-{}-allow-partial-bank} escape exists for smoke-testing; it prints
a pre-registration warning and marks output \texttt{exploratory\_only},
and nothing produced under it may be reported. On completion day, 171/171
tests plus the D1/D3/D-aux selftests passed (2026-07-20), and the interlock
HELD: no real-bank statistic was computed before 480/480.

\paragraph{Dated amendments, never silent revisions.} The one amendment
this campaign required is the D3 pair design. The card left the pair count
and stratification open, requiring declaration before labels exist. The
pre-declaration was filed minutes after bank completion, while zero D3
pairs, merges, or labels existed: $N = 120$ vertex-disjoint pairs per
family (\texttt{max\_run\_uses} $= 1$, the dyadic-dependence-safe design),
$\alpha = 0.5$ midpoint merges, stratified over task-pair cells. It was
amended the same day---still before any pair, merge, or label existed,
with the artifact-anchored timeline in the record---because the frozen,
adversarially reviewed sampler is uniform without replacement and has no
per-cell stratification mode; amending the declaration was the lesser
deviation versus modifying frozen analysis code after the bank existed.
The Director's ruling on review: temporal integrity holds, the amendment
is the conservative choice, ``clean dated amendment; no objection.''

All five analyses ran against the real bank exactly once, on completion

\subsection{H1: the canonicalization ceiling}
\label{sec:h1}

H1 asks whether an adapter's weights identify the task it was trained on,
within a family. The classifier is deliberately weak---a leave-one-out
% SOURCE: C:/falco/rhombic/paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 516-519, 790-792
% (SVM C = 1.0; 1,000-shuffle permutation null; 240 adapters per family).
linear SVM ($C = 1.0$) over 240 adapters per family with a 1{,}000-shuffle
permutation null---so that the representation, not model capacity, carries
the result. The pre-registered lock is two-sided: LOO accuracy above
1.5$\times$ chance (0.2500, at chance 0.1667) \emph{and} permutation
$p < 0.01$. Four representations per family: raw flattened weights
(6{,}541{,}248 dimensions Qwen, 5{,}114{,}112 Llama); the
$\mathrm{GL}(r)$-gauge-canonical representation in the
QR$\rightarrow$SVD lineage (88{,}704 / 50{,}688); and two vocab-signature
variants computed through the base model's unembedding (15{,}232 / 8{,}704
including k/v; 7{,}616 / 4{,}352 excluding).

The pattern is stark and consistent (Table~\ref{tab:h1}). Raw weights---the
highest-dimensional representation---fail the lock at or below chance,
while the canonical representation identifies all six tasks perfectly, as
do both vocab-signature variants, at a fraction of the dimensionality.
Three certifications guard the reading. First, raw's tiny permutation $p$
alongside sub-threshold accuracy is not a contradiction: under LOO, label
permutation drives accuracy \emph{below} chance (raw nulls center 0.012
qwen / 0.057 llama, maxima 0.054 / 0.121), so both raw arms exceed their
null maxima---raw carries a \emph{weak} recoverable signal (code recall
0.45 for qwen with every other class $\le 0.025$; alpaca 0.425 plus code
0.40 for llama with every other class at 0) while coming nowhere near the
1.5$\times$ bar. This is exactly the case the two-sided lock was built to
reject, and we report the failure as a failure. Second, the pre-registered
variance-heterogeneity guard reads ratios of 1.00--1.46 against a trigger
of 3.7---not triggered; the ceiling is not a variance artifact. Third, the
Director re-derived all eight LOO accuracies from the confusion matrices,
then re-ran the canonical LOO-SVM from scratch on the feature matrices,
obtaining 1.0000 for both families.

The reading: task identity lives in the adapter weights but becomes
legible only once the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$ gauge is removed; the
vocab-signature arm matches canonical at ceiling, so the result is not
specific to one canonicalization route. Within a controlled family at
coarse label granularity, a linear probe suffices once the gauge is
gone---canonicalization, not classifier capacity, is the binding
constraint. The scope is exactly that: six coarse tasks in a controlled
family; we claim nothing about fine-grained label regimes
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{H2: the pre-registered prediction, refuted by its own control}
\label{sec:h2}

H2 was our boldest pre-registered prediction, and it was wrong. We
predicted that task structure would \emph{not} transfer across model
families. The verdict is \textbf{NOT supported}---and the refutation was
produced by our own triviality control, added in round-1 review before
any data existed.

Two dimension-agnostic representations carry the analysis: depth-binned
singular-value spectra of the effective update (spectrum, the pinned
primary; 384 dimensions) and a canonicalized probe-projection (probe,
corroborating; 12{,}672 dimensions), under the pinned decision rule of
Section~\ref{sec:prereg}. The triviality control asks, before reading any
transfer number, whether a classifier can tell which family an adapter
came from: on raw representations the family-identity probe reads 1.0000
(chance 0.5000), so a classifier trained on one family and tested on the
other is tested under total covariate shift---raw ``transfer-at-chance''
would confirm our prediction while measuring nothing but family scale.
Unsupervised per-family z-standardization (which never touches task labels
and so cannot manufacture task structure; the Director confirmed this in
source) collapses the probe to 0.1521 (spectrum) / 0.0000 (probe)---below
binary chance, the expected signature of standardizing on the full pool
before cross-validation, and the transfer verdict rests on the binomial
tests of the transfer accuracies themselves, not on the probe reading
chance.

With the artifact removed, the picture inverts (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}).
Raw transfer sits at or near chance in every direction---the number that
would have ``confirmed'' the prediction. Standardized transfer runs
0.7375--0.7833 across a 1.5B and a 1B model of different lineages, with
binomial $p$ between $7.70\times10^{-98}$ and $1.20\times10^{-84}$.
Condition (i) of the pinned rule fails decisively in both directions, in
both representations; spectrum and probe agree. One caveat on the $p$
magnitudes: the exact-binomial test treats the per-direction test
predictions as independent, but they cluster by task, so the printed
$p$-values are anti-conservative; the verdict does not lean on the
exponents---accuracies of 0.74--0.78 against chance 0.1667 clear
$\alpha = 0.01$ under any clustering-robust reading.
% SOURCE: C:/falco/rhombic/paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 975-983
% (clustering caveat; 0.74-0.78 robustness phrasing).
Because a pre-registration that flips via a control added in review
carries the highest motivated-reasoning risk in the set, the Director
re-ran the entire H2 pipeline end-to-end from the spectrum and probe
feature matrices, applying the standardization independently: the
triviality control and all four standardized transfer accuracies, raw
baselines, and binomial $p$-values reproduce to the digit.

Had we run H2 without the triviality control, we would now be reporting a
confirmed prediction that is actually a measurement of family scale. The
cost of the control was one additional probe and a standardization pass;
the benefit was the difference between a false confirmation and a true
refutation. That asymmetry, not the transfer number itself, is what this
subsection is evidence for.

\subsection{D2: the identity backbone is the sole load-bearing structure}
\label{sec:d2}

D2 measures what the trained bridge is worth at inference time by
transplanting bridges between adapters and measuring the val-loss
consequence. The locked hypothesis (H3) is directional with opposite
predictions: the \emph{substrate} hypothesis says a cross-task bridge swap
costs on the order of the cross-seed baseline; the \emph{task-specific}
hypothesis says the cross-task penalty is large and scales with between-task
distance. Seven swap kinds are evaluated, including the two permutation
cells that carry the structural contrast: \texttt{permuted\_deviation}
($I + \mathrm{permute}(B - I)$, the pinned H3 reference, preserving the
identity backbone) and full-entry permutation (a shared derangement of all
36 entry positions, destroying it); the two share the same derangement per
(family, task, slot), so their difference isolates the backbone effect.
% SOURCE: C:/falco/rhombic/paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 588-591 (K = 3
% donor/recipient pairs per cell, amortized natives).
With $K = 3$ donor/recipient pairs per cell this gives 360 evaluations per
family (18 native references; penalty cells $n = 90$ for the cross-task,
magnitude, and topology kinds, $n = 18$ elsewhere), each re-assembled from
the bank and verified against its recorded assembly SHA-256 before
evaluation; all passed.

Installing a different task's trained bridge costs essentially nothing

supported (cross-task penalties sit at the cross-seed baseline) and the
task-specific hypothesis is refuted; the cross-task penalty neither is
large nor scales with between-task distance (both decomposition cells,
run unconditionally by Director ruling rather than gated on a
data-dependent trigger, are ${\sim}0$). The scope is in-distribution val
loss under the house bridge architecture. The Director's regrade
reproduced all 14 per-kind means exactly from the per-eval rows.

\subsection{D3: post-hoc, weight-only merge-conflict prediction}
\label{sec:d3}

D3 asks whether two adapters' weights alone---post hoc, with no access to
training---predict whether their merge degrades. The training-time form of
merge-conflict prediction belongs to \cite{mergeprobe2026}; D3 is the
weight-only, post-hoc regime---two finished adapters, no training access,
no activations, no data---and we claim only this regime. Weight-only
conflict \emph{heuristics} (sign agreement, task-vector geometry) predate
it \cite{yadav2023ties,ilharco2023task}; D3's contribution is the
supervised, held-out-evaluated form. The quantity predicted---whether the
$\alpha = 0.5$ midpoint interpolation degrades endpoint loss---is the loss
barrier of the linear-mode-connectivity literature
\cite{frankle2020lmc,ainsworth2023rebasin}.

\paragraph{Design and labels.} $N = 120$ vertex-disjoint pairs per family
(each run used at most once), $\alpha = 0.5$ midpoint merges, 240 merged
adapters GPU-evaluated on both endpoint tasks' fixed val splits; the pair
design's dated amendment is Section~\ref{sec:prereg}, and the realized
same-task coverage is reported descriptively: 14/120 (11.7\%) qwen and
22/120 (18.3\%) llama against ${\sim}16\%$ (${\sim}20$/120) expected under
uniformity. The pinned label rule: a pair is a conflict if the merge
degrades either endpoint by $\ge 5\%$ in relative perplexity, with a 10\%
degenerate-balance floor triggering a pre-declared fallback (not used).
The pooled conflict rate is 85.8\%; per family, qwen 99 conflicts / 21
non-conflicts (82.5\%) and llama 107 / 13 (89.2\%)---llama's minority
fraction (10.8\%) clears the floor by a single pair, so its AUC rests on
13 negative pairs. A conflict rate this high is itself a finding about
midpoint merging in this bank.

\paragraph{Model and result.} Logistic regression under group-aware
cross-validation (StratifiedGroupKFold, fold seed 0, 5 splits,
$n_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1000$), comparing a 2-feature distance baseline
(cosine and $L_2$ distance) against the full weight-only set (those two,
four gauge-invariant principal-angle aggregates, and two per-module
vectors).

\begin{table}[ht]

Two adapters' weights alone predict whether their midpoint merge degrades
an endpoint, and the gauge-invariant block adds a margin over raw distance
whose CI excludes zero in both families (Table~\ref{tab:d3}). Three
certifications bound the claim. First, the distance-only baseline is
fold-scheme-sensitive: the Director's independent from-scratch re-run
under the naive fold scheme reproduced the report's naive block
exactly---distance 0.686 (qwen) / 0.667 (llama), full 0.995 /
0.952---against the group-aware headline baseline of 0.675 / 0.713; the
${\sim}0.01$--0.05 gap is fold-scheme variance, not seed noise, which is
why the fold configuration is pinned and the fragility reported rather
than averaged away. Second, the conflict label is strongly associated with
task composition (negatives are dominated by same-task pairs: 13 of 21
qwen, 9 of 13 llama), and H1 shows task identity is fully recoverable from
weight features; a task-composition oracle alone reaches AUC 0.804 (qwen)
/ 0.785 (llama)---already above the distance baseline. The full model
exceeds the oracle bound, so geometric signal beyond task identity is
present, but the headline AUC upper-bounds the predictor's discriminative
value within the cross-task-only regime, which was not separately
pre-registered; we bound it rather than claim it. Third, invertible
bridges are a gauge on the update column space, so the principal-angle
features are provably insensitive to bridge numerics---the margin over
distance is evidence about update geometry, not about the bridge,
consistent with D2.

\subsection{D-aux: the pilot correlation shrinks honestly}
\label{sec:daux}

D-aux re-verifies, at bank scale, a pilot correlation of $r = 0.888$
between bridge deviation (\texttt{dev\_mean}, mean over modules of
$\lVert \mathrm{bridge}_{\mathrm{final}} - I \rVert_F$) and generalization
gap (val minus train loss at the final step)---the re-verification itself
pinned in the Director decisions before the bank completed. A step-0
identity control must read exactly 0.0 by construction; it did.
Within-task cells are the pre-registered Simpson's-paradox guard:
descriptive, but shown, not hidden.

The pilot's $r = 0.888$ does not survive the bank, and we report that as
the result (Table~\ref{tab:daux}). Pooled over 480 runs the association is

small-$n$ and task mixture---and heterogeneous within task: math is
positive in both families while xsum in qwen is significantly negative. A
deviation measure whose within-task sign flips between cells is not a
universal overfitting signal; it is a between-task association with real
but task-dependent within-task structure. The shrink is a success of the
protocol, not a failure of the result: a pre-registered re-verification at
scale is exactly the check that separates a real-but-modest association
from an artifact of the sample that first suggested it. The Director's
regrade reproduced the pooled and per-family values from the 480 per-run
pairs. Read jointly with D2: the bridge deviation carries information
\emph{about} the run without carrying the in-distribution task
solution---a diagnostic-bearing structure, not a load-bearing one.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The gauge, not the information, was the obstacle.} The two
identifiability results share one mechanism. In H1, raw weights fail the
lock while the canonical representation reaches ceiling at a fraction of
the dimensionality; raw's weak recoverable trace shows the signal was in
the weights the whole time, buried by the factorization gauge. In H2 the
same story repeats one level up: raw cross-family transfer sat at chance,
and the triviality control revealed why---family identity is read from raw
features at 1.0000, so raw ``transfer failure'' was family-scale covariate
shift, not absent structure. One reading covers both: nuisance
variation---the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$ factorization gauge within a family, the
scale gauge between families---dominates raw weight coordinates and
obscures task structure that is intact underneath. Task identity is
gauge-obscured, not absent. The scope runs on the label-granularity axis:
% SOURCE: C:/falco/rhombic/paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 241-244,
% 1336-1340 (W2T regime contrast: 312 binary attributes, 10k+ adapters).
our six coarse tasks are the opposite regime from W2T's fine-grained
attribute label spaces (312 binary attributes over 10k+-adapter
collections) \cite{w2t2026lora}, and we make no claim about what a linear
probe recovers, raw or canonical, at that granularity---the two results
are complements on that axis, not contradictions. Our canonical
representation follows the QR$\rightarrow$SVD readout lineage;
\cite{putterman2024lol} identified the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$ symmetry and
built equivariant processors for it, and \cite{paul2026spectral} reads
training objective from adapter spectra. H1 places a point on this map:
at this operating point, explicit gauge removal plus a linear SVM is
already at ceiling.

\paragraph{What pre-registration bought.} Two of the five results went
against us, and those two are the credibility of the other three. The H2
prediction was refuted specifically by the control added in review:
without the family-identity probe, raw transfer-at-chance would have read
as a confirmation, and it would have been false. A pre-registration that
flips via its own control is the protocol working as designed, not
failing. The D-aux pilot correlation shrank from 0.888 to 0.300 at bank
scale, with the pre-declared guard cells exposing the within-task
heterogeneity---including a significantly negative cell---rather than
averaging it away. A pipeline that can only confirm is not measuring;
this one refuted its boldest prediction and deflated its own pilot, under
an interlock that made post-hoc rescue impossible. The run log stays in
the record for the same reason (Section~\ref{sec:verification}):
maker--grader separation was applied exactly where the pipeline was
newest.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Cost first: the bank consumed roughly 17 days of
a single local GPU; the analyses themselves are cheap, but the artifact
they require is not. Two families at 1--1.5B scale; whether the
canonicalization ceiling, backbone dominance, or angle-based merge
prediction hold at larger scale or across architecture classes is
unmeasured. Six coarse tasks; ceiling accuracy also means H1 cannot rank
representations above the lock. In-distribution val loss only---the D2
conclusion is explicitly in-distribution, and D-aux's $r = 0.300$ hints
the deviation matters for something val loss does not fully capture.
Single merge operator ($\alpha = 0.5$ midpoint); other $\alpha$ values,
TIES/DARE-style operators \cite{yadav2023ties,yu2023dare}, and merges of
more than two adapters are outside the measured claim. Uniform pair
sampling by dated amendment, with uneven realized cell coverage reported
descriptively; the headline AUC is a claim about the uniform pair
population, not any single cell. The D3 distance baseline is
fold-scheme-sensitive, and the small negative class (21 / 13 pairs) is
reflected in the CIs. One bridge architecture and one training recipe:
the bank varies only family, task, and seed, so recipe-coupled effects
are unmeasured, and standard bridgeless LoRA is outside the D2/D-aux
measured claim. The D3 headline upper-bounds discriminative value within
the cross-task-only regime (Section~\ref{sec:d3}).

\section{Reproducibility and released artifacts}
\label{sec:repro}

Every headline number is re-derivable from a per-item verification bundle,
and every one has already been re-derived by someone who was not us. The
bundle carries two tiers: Tier 1 is per-item result data (per-family
confusion matrices and permutation summaries; the full H2 transfer and
family-identity-probe tables; 360 per-eval D2 rows per family with
assembly SHA-256s; 240 per-pair D3 feature dicts and labels; 480 per-run
D-aux pairs); Tier 2 is the reduced feature matrices
(\texttt{features\_<family>.npz}, 90.6 MB qwen / 56.7 MB llama, float32),
exported by the frozen feature functions so a from-scratch classifier
re-run bit-matches. The bundle is anchored at rhombic commit
\texttt{638f4a8}, archive SHA-256
{\small\texttt{c1891d50fc7c48c27d6fb606d667f1f1120105ed55cb7414c8e4b3d874acfadd}},
all 16 files matching the SHA256SUMS manifest byte-for-byte; the Director
regrade worked from exactly those bytes. Upstream, the interlock and the
171/171-test tooling verification are themselves reproducibility
mechanisms: all five analyses ran against the finished bank exactly once,
with every pinned choice recorded in each tool's output JSON.

The verification bundle and all analysis code ship in the public rhombic
repository (\url{https://github.com/tasumermaf/rhombic}); the reduced
feature matrices (${\sim}147$ MB) are published on Hugging Face
(\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/timotheospaul/rhombic-asset1-features});
the adversarial-audit ledger with all 117 dispositioned findings is in the
repository under \texttt{paper/audit/}. The adapter bank itself (480
trained runs) remains a local artifact for size; its manifest, per-run
configs, and the trainer that reproduces it are in the repository. The
