\begin{abstract}
Raw flattened weights are the unexamined default of adapter analysis; we
measure what the default costs. Against a pre-registered bank of 480 LoRA
adapters---2 model families $\times$ 6 tasks $\times$ 40 seeds---raw weights
fail a locked task-identifiability test (leave-one-out accuracy 0.0792 /
0.1375, chance 0.1667), while $\mathrm{GL}(r)$-gauge-canonical and
vocabulary-signature representations identify all six tasks at 1.0000
(permutation $p = 0.000999$). Our pre-registered prediction that task
structure would \emph{not} transfer across families was refuted by its own
control: raw transfer-at-chance was a family-scale covariate-shift artifact
(family-identity probe 1.0000 on raw features), and shift-controlled
transfer runs 0.7375--0.7833 (binomial $p \le 1.20\times10^{-84}$). One
mechanism covers both: nuisance variation---the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$
factorization gauge within a family, the scale gauge between
families---dominates raw coordinates; task identity is gauge-obscured, not
absent. Companion results: every bridge swap preserving the identity
backbone costs under $+0.001$ nats, while destroying it costs $+2.8086$ /
$+3.8365$; weight-only features predict midpoint-merge conflict at
group-aware AUC 0.995 / 0.962; a pilot correlation of $r = 0.888$ shrinks
honestly to 0.300. Hypotheses were locked before the bank existed, each
analysis ran exactly once, every headline was independently re-derived, and
two of five outcomes went against us.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result, in ninety seconds}
\label{sec:hook}

A trained LoRA adapter is a small stack of low-rank matrices, and adapters
now accumulate by the thousands---shared on hubs, merged into products,
composed by people who never saw the training run. Given only the adapter
tensors---no training data, no logs, no access to the training
process---what can be read out? The obstacle is not information but
parameterization: a LoRA update factorizes as $BA$, and for any invertible
$r \times r$ matrix $M$ the reparameterization $(B, A) \rightarrow (BM,
M^{-1}A)$ leaves the effective update unchanged---the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$
gauge. Raw flattened weights are the unexamined default of adapter
analysis, and this paper measures what the default costs.

The answer, measured once against a pre-registered 480-adapter bank: the
default costs legibility itself. Raw adapter weights fail a locked
task-identifiability test at leave-one-out accuracy 0.0792 (Qwen) / 0.1375
(Llama) against chance 0.1667---while the same adapters, canonicalized to
remove the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$ gauge, identify all six training tasks at
1.0000 in both families (permutation $p = 0.000999$), at a small fraction
of the raw dimensionality. One level up, the identical story: our own
pre-registered prediction that task structure would \emph{not} transfer
across model families was refuted by its own triviality control---raw
transfer-at-chance was a covariate-shift artifact of family scale, and once
an unsupervised per-family standardization removes it, task structure
transfers between a 1.5B and a 1B model of different lineages at
0.7375--0.7833. Task identity is gauge-obscured, not absent. The
canonicalization, not classifier capacity, is the binding
constraint---scoped to coarse label granularity within a controlled family
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Why a reader deciding quickly should care: (i) if you triage finished
adapters post hoc---provenance, transfer, mergeability---canonicalize
before you conclude anything; a linear probe on gauge-fixed features
suffices where the raw default fails; (ii) the same weight-only regime
supports a supervised merge-conflict predictor at group-aware AUC 0.995 /
0.962 with a CI-separated margin over raw distance; and (iii) the numbers
arrive with unusual evidentiary standing---hypotheses locked before the
bank existed, every open choice pinned in dated rulings, a completeness
interlock that held, each analysis run exactly once, every headline
independently re-derived by a separate Director instance, and two of the
five pre-registered outcomes decided \emph{against} the authors
(Section~\ref{sec:methods}).

\section{The measurement}
\label{sec:measurement}

\subsection{The instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}

The artifact is a bank of 480 LoRA adapters: 2 model families $\times$ 6
tasks $\times$ 40 seeds (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct;
tasks alpaca, code, math, xsum, squad, agnews). Every run trains a rank-24
adapter on the q/k/v/o projections with a 6-channel identity-initialized
bridge per wrapped module (effective update $\Delta W = B\,(M \otimes
I_4)\,A$; at identity initialization the bridge is exactly absent and the
update is standard LoRA): AdamW at peak LR 0.0002, weight decay 0.01, clip
1.0, linear warmup (100 steps) then cosine decay to a 0.1 floor; 2000
optimizer steps at effective batch 16 ($4 \times 4$) $=$ 32{,}000
sequences of length 512; per-task pools capped at 40{,}000 examples and a
fixed 500-example validation split per task (seed 777); bfloat16 base,
float32 adapters. Adapter module counts are discovered from each adapter
rather than hardcoded: 112 modules per run for Qwen, 64 for Llama. The
campaign ran on a single local GPU from July 3 21:52Z to July 20
16:21Z---roughly 17 days including one restart---and the manifest closed at
480/480 COMPLETE on 2026-07-20T16:21:19Z; the outage ledger contains
exactly four failures, all HF Hub 504 errors on July 16 (run indices
329--332), all retried to COMPLETE. Chance accuracy for the 6-task
classification problems throughout is 0.1667.

\subsection{H1: task identity is legible only after canonicalization}
\label{sec:h1}

H1 asks whether an adapter's weights identify the task it was trained on,
within a family. The classifier is deliberately weak---a leave-one-out
% SOURCE: paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 518-519, 790-791 (C = 1.0,
% 1,000-shuffle permutation null, 240 adapters/family = 6 tasks x 40 seeds).

the result. The pre-registered lock is two-sided: LOO accuracy above

representations were computed per family: raw flattened weights; the
$\mathrm{GL}(r)$-gauge-canonical representation (QR$\rightarrow$SVD
lineage, bridge absorbed); and two pre-registered vocab-signature variants
(output-referenced through the base model's unembedding), with and without
k/v modules.

The pattern is stark and consistent (Table~\ref{tab:h1}). Raw adapter

artifact. The Director re-derived all eight LOO accuracies from the
confusion matrices and re-ran the canonical LOO-SVM from scratch on the
feature matrices, obtaining 1.0000 for both families.

\subsection{H2: the pre-registered prediction, refuted by its own control}
\label{sec:h2}

H2 was our boldest pre-registered prediction, and it was wrong. We
predicted that task structure would \emph{not} transfer across model
families. The verdict is \textbf{NOT supported}---and the refutation was
produced by our own triviality control, added in round-1 review before any
data existed.

Two dimension-agnostic representations carry the analysis: depth-binned
singular-value spectra of the effective update (\textbf{spectrum}, the
pinned primary; 384 dimensions) and a canonicalized probe-projection
(\textbf{probe}, corroborating; 12{,}672 dimensions). The decision rule was
pinned by the Director before the bank completed: H2 (transfer fails) is
supported iff, for both directions in the shift-controlled representation,
(i) cross-family accuracy is not significantly above chance at one-sided
exact-binomial $\alpha = 0.01$, and (ii) within-family accuracy exceeds
cross-family accuracy by $\ge 15$ percentage points.

The triviality control asks, before reading any transfer number, whether a
classifier can tell \emph{which family} an adapter came from. On raw
representations it can, perfectly: family-identity probe 1.0000 (spectrum
and probe, chance 0.5000). A classifier trained on one family and tested
on the other is therefore tested under total covariate shift---raw
``transfer-at-chance'' would confirm our prediction while measuring
nothing but family scale. Unsupervised per-family z-standardization
(task labels never touched, so it has no capacity to manufacture task
structure) collapses the probe to 0.1521 (spectrum) / 0.0000 (probe);
both sit below binary chance, the signature of standardizing on the full
pool before cross-validation---the family-\emph{mean} separation the raw
probe exploited is gone, and the transfer verdict rests on the binomial
tests of the transfer accuracies themselves.

With the artifact removed, the picture inverts (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}).
Raw transfer sits at or near chance in every direction---the number that
would have ``confirmed'' the prediction. Standardized transfer runs
0.7375--0.7833 across a 1.5B and a 1B model of different lineages, with
binomial $p \le 1.20\times10^{-84}$; spectrum and probe agree. One caveat:
% SOURCE: paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 976-983 (clustered predictions;
% anti-conservative p caveat; 240 test predictions per direction).
the exact-binomial test treats the 240 test predictions per direction as
independent, but they cluster by task, so the printed $p$-values are
anti-conservative; the verdict does not lean on the exponents---accuracies
of 0.74--0.78 against chance 0.1667 clear $\alpha = 0.01$ under any
clustering-robust reading, and the $\ge 15$pp margin condition fails
independently of the test. Because a pre-registration that flips via a
control added in review carries the highest motivated-reasoning risk in the
set, the Director re-ran the entire H2 pipeline end-to-end from the feature
matrices, applying the standardization independently: every value
reproduced to the digit. Had we run H2 without the control, we would now be
reporting a confirmed prediction that is actually a measurement of family
scale.

\section{What it means}
\label{sec:meaning}

\paragraph{One mechanism, two results.} Nuisance variation---the
$\mathrm{GL}(r)$ factorization gauge within a family, the scale gauge
between families---dominates raw weight coordinates and obscures task
structure that is intact underneath. Task identity is gauge-obscured, not
absent. The scope of this claim runs on the label-granularity axis: our six
% SOURCE: paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 241-243, 1336-1340 (W2T regime:
% 312-binary-attribute label space, 10k+-adapter collections).
coarse tasks are the opposite regime from W2T's fine-grained attribute
label spaces (312 binary attributes over 10k+-adapter collections)
\cite{w2t2026lora}, and we make no claim about what a linear probe
recovers, raw or canonical, at that granularity. Within our regime the
statement is sharp: no learned encoder is needed---explicit gauge removal
plus a linear SVM is already at ceiling, and the same features left in the
raw gauge fail a 1.5$\times$-chance lock. This complements, rather than
contradicts, the readout-at-scale line: weight-space readout of training
provenance predates LoRA \cite{eilertsen2020classify,unterthiner2020predict},
W2T \cite{w2t2026lora} established LoRA checkpoints as legible objects
under QR$\rightarrow$SVD canonicalization at fine granularity, Learning on
LoRAs \cite{putterman2024lol} identified the $\mathrm{GL}(r)$ symmetry and
built equivariant processors for it, and spectral readouts
\cite{paul2026spectral} classify training objective from adapter spectra.
Both ends of the granularity axis agree that the raw parameterization is
the obstacle.

\subsection{The identity backbone is the sole load-bearing structure (D2/H3)}
\label{sec:d2}

D2 measures what the trained bridge is worth at inference time by
transplanting bridges between adapters and measuring the val-loss
consequence, under a locked directional hypothesis: the \emph{substrate}
hypothesis (swaps cost little) against the \emph{task-specific} hypothesis
% SOURCE: paper/rhombic-asset1.tex line 589 (K = 3 donor/recipient pairs
% per cell) and lines 600-607 (permutation-cell construction, 36 entries).
(cross-task penalties are large and scale with task distance). $K = 3$
donor/recipient pairs per cell give 360 evaluations per family (18 native
references; penalty cells $n = 90$ for cross-task/magnitude/topology,
$n = 18$ elsewhere), every one re-assembled from the bank and SHA-verified
before evaluation; all passed. Seven kinds are evaluated, of which two
carry the structural contrast: \texttt{permuted\_deviation} scrambles the
trained deviation while preserving the identity backbone; the full-entry
permutation (a shared derangement of all 36 bridge-entry positions)
destroys the backbone. The two share the same derangement per cell, so
their difference isolates the backbone effect and nothing else.

\subsection{Post-hoc, weight-only merge-conflict prediction (D3)}
\label{sec:d3}

D3 asks whether two adapters' weights alone---no training access, no
activations, no data---predict whether their merge degrades. The regime
matters: the training-time form of merge-conflict prediction is owned by
\cite{mergeprobe2026}; D3 claims only the weight-only, post-hoc regime, and
weight-only conflict \emph{heuristics} (sign agreement, task-vector
geometry) predate it \cite{yadav2023ties,ilharco2023task}. The quantity
predicted---whether the $\alpha = 0.5$ midpoint interpolation degrades
endpoint loss---is the loss barrier of the linear-mode-connectivity
literature \cite{frankle2020lmc,ainsworth2023rebasin}.

Design: $N = 120$ vertex-disjoint pairs per family (each run used at most
once---the dyadic-dependence-safe design), $\alpha = 0.5$ midpoint merges,
240 merged adapters GPU-evaluated on both endpoints. Label rule, pinned
before the bank completed: conflict iff the merge degrades either endpoint
by $\ge 5\%$ in relative perplexity, with a 10\% degenerate-balance floor
(fallback not used). The realized conflict rate is itself a finding about
midpoint merging in this bank: pooled 85.8\%; qwen 99 conflicts / 21
non-conflicts (82.5\%), llama 107 / 13 (89.2\%), llama's minority fraction
(10.8\%) clearing the floor by a single pair. Features:
% SOURCE: paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 636-650 (feature-set contents:
% 2 distance scalars; 4 principal-angle aggregates; per-module vectors of
% length 112/64).
a 2-scalar \texttt{distance} baseline (cosine and $\ell_2$) against a
\texttt{full} weight-only set adding 4 gauge-invariant principal-angle
aggregates and two per-module vectors (length 112 Qwen / 64 Llama).
Classifier: logistic regression, group-aware StratifiedGroupKFold, fold
seed 0, 5 splits, 1000 bootstrap replicates---all pinned.

Two adapters' weights alone predict whether their midpoint merge degrades
an endpoint at AUC 0.96--0.99, and the gauge-invariant block adds a margin
over raw distance whose CI excludes zero in both families
(Table~\ref{tab:d3}). Two caveats bind the headline. First, the
distance-only baseline is fold-scheme-sensitive: the Director's independent
from-scratch re-run under the naive scheme reproduced the naive block
exactly (distance 0.686 / 0.667, full 0.995 / 0.952) against the
group-aware headline baseline of 0.675 / 0.713---a ${\sim}0.01$--0.05
fold-scheme gap on a 2-feature model, which is why the fold configuration
is pinned and the fragility reported. Second, the conflict label is
strongly associated with task composition (negatives are dominated by
same-task pairs: 13 of 21 qwen, 9 of 13 llama), and H1 shows task identity
is fully recoverable from weight features; a task-composition oracle alone
reaches AUC 0.804 / 0.785---above the distance baseline---so the headline
AUC upper-bounds the predictor's discriminative value within the
cross-task-only regime. The full model exceeds the oracle bound, so
geometric signal beyond task identity is present, but we bound the
within-regime value rather than claim it.

\subsection{The pilot correlation shrinks honestly (D-aux)}
\label{sec:daux}

D-aux re-verifies, at bank scale, a pilot correlation of $r = 0.888$
between bridge deviation (\texttt{dev\_mean}) and generalization gap
(\texttt{final\_gap})---the re-verification itself pinned in the Director
decisions before the bank completed. A step-0 identity control must read
exactly 0.0 by construction (identity-initialized bridge); it did.

overfitting signal. Read with D2, the conjunction is the finding: the
trained bridge deviation carries information \emph{about} the run (enough
for a weak correlate of its generalization gap) without carrying the
in-distribution task solution, which lives in the identity backbone. The
bridge is a diagnostic-bearing structure, not a load-bearing one.

\section{Methods, compressed: pre-registration with teeth}
\label{sec:methods}

Three layers, each with a paper trail. (1)~\emph{The locked card}: the
hypotheses (H1, H2, H3, the D3 prediction target, the D-aux
re-verification) were locked in the experiment card of 2026-07-03, before
the bank existed. (2)~\emph{Director-pinned decisions}: every analytical
choice the card left open was pinned in a dated ruling by an independent
Director instance on 2026-07-06, before the bank completed---the H2
decision rule, the D2 structure reference (with the magnitude/topology
decomposition cells run unconditionally, so that no data-dependent forking
point exists), and the D3 label rule; the vocab-signature D1 arm entered as
a dated amendment. The one post-completion declaration, the D3 pair design,
was filed 2026-07-20 before any pairs, merges, or labels existed, amended
the same day (still before any pair artifact) from per-cell stratification
to the uniform vertex-disjoint sampler the frozen tool actually implements,
and ratified by the Director as a clean dated amendment; the realized
% SOURCE: paper/rhombic-asset1.tex lines 685-686, 1453-1457 (21 unordered
% task-pair cells; uneven realized coverage).
coverage of the 21 task-pair cells is reported descriptively (same-task
pairs 14/120 = 11.7\% qwen, 22/120 = 18.3\% llama, against ${\sim}16\%$
$\approx$ 20/120 expected under uniformity). (3)~\emph{The completeness
interlock}: every CLI that can touch the real bank calls
\texttt{require\_complete\_bank()} before reading a single adapter, passing
only at 480/480 COMPLETE in the manifest. The tooling passed 171/171 tests
plus the D1/D3/D-aux selftests on 2026-07-20, and the interlock HELD: no
real-bank statistic was computed before 480/480. Each analysis then ran
against the real bank exactly once.

Verification is maker--grader separation at three levels: fresh-context
adversarial review of every custom component before it touched data (the
D3 labels runner, written post-card, was verified before its GPU run,

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Cost.} The bank consumed roughly 17 days of a single local GPU
% SOURCE: paper/rhombic-asset1.tex line 1564 (NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada, 48 GB).
(an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada, 48\,GB); measured per-run rates are 30.56 min/run
(llama3.2-1b, $n = 240$), 51.72 min/run (qwen2.5-1.5b, $n = 240$), blended
41.14 min/run over all 480 [measured]. The analyses are cheap---D1, D-aux,
and D3 feature extraction are CPU-only---but the artifact they require is
not.

\textbf{Scope.} Two families at 1--1.5B scale; whether the ceilings hold
at 7B+ or across architecture classes is unmeasured. Six coarse tasks: the
label-granularity axis cuts both ways---we claim nothing about fine-grained
regimes, and ceiling accuracy on six classes means H1 cannot rank
representations above the lock. In-distribution val loss only: D2's verdict
is explicitly about this loss; behavioral and out-of-distribution
consequences of swaps and merges are unmeasured. Single merge operator:
$\alpha = 0.5$ midpoints only; other $\alpha$, TIES/DARE-style operators,
and $>$2-adapter merges are outside the measured claim. One bridge
architecture and one training recipe: D2 and D-aux are properties of the
house identity-initialized, controller-free bridge, and every adapter
shares a single hyperparameter configuration; heterogeneous banks could
couple recipe to task and change every result.

\textbf{Statistical fragilities, reported where they appear.} The D3
distance baseline moves ${\sim}0.01$--0.05 with fold scheme; the fold
configuration is pinned. The D3 negative class is small (21 qwen / 13
llama non-conflict pairs; llama clears the 10\% floor by a single pair),
and the task-composition oracle (AUC 0.804 / 0.785) bounds how much of the
headline is task identification. The D-aux pooled $r = 0.300$ conceals
sign-varying within-task structure ($n = 40$ per cell); any within-task use
would need task-specific calibration the bank is only pilot-scale for.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every headline number is re-derivable from a per-item verification bundle,
and every one has already been re-derived by someone who was not us. The
bundle carries per-item result data (confusion matrices and permutation
summaries behind every H1 accuracy; the full H2 transfer and
family-identity tables; 360 per-eval D2 rows per family with assembly
SHA-256s; 240 per-pair D3 feature dicts and labels; 480 per-run D-aux
pairs) and the reduced feature matrices
(\texttt{features\_<family>.npz}, 90.6\,MB qwen / 56.7\,MB llama, float32),
exported by the frozen feature functions so a from-scratch classifier
re-run bit-matches. The bundle is anchored at rhombic commit
\texttt{638f4a8}, archive SHA-256
{\small\texttt{c1891d50fc7c48c27d6fb606d667f1f1120105ed55cb7414c8e4b3d874acfadd}},
all 16 files matching the SHA256SUMS manifest byte-for-byte. The
verification bundle and all analysis code ship in the public repository
(\url{https://github.com/tasumermaf/rhombic}); the feature matrices
(${\sim}147$\,MB total) are published as a Hugging Face dataset linked from
the repository README. The adapter bank itself remains a local artifact for
size; its manifest, per-run configs, and the trainer that reproduces it are
in the repository. The parent paper (\texttt{paper/rhombic-asset1.tex} in
the same repository) carries the full design, decision record, and audit
trail this condensation compresses.
