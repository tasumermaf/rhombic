## Part 3: The rank-fraction confound

This is the substantive finding of my pass, and it is not in either draft. **The H2 design holds LoRA rank fixed at 24 while model width varies by 2.3x, so "transfer decays with scale" and "a fixed-rank adapter expresses a thinner slice of a wider matrix" are the same observation.**

The numbers, from configs I fetched: rank 24 against hidden size is **0.0156** at the Qwen 1.5B anchor, **0.0117** at 3B, and **0.0067** at 7B. The rank fraction falls **2.33x** across the within-lineage scale ladder that H2-S is built on. Worse for interpretation, the KV projections are narrower and shrink discontinuously with the GQA layout: r/kv_dim is 0.0938 at both 1.5B and 3B, then halves to 0.0469 at 7B. So the spectrum representation's q/k/v/o blocks do not mean the same thing across the ladder.

**Consequence for the pre-registered outcomes.** H2-S outcome (A), transfer holds at or above the band across all scale pairs, remains clean and interpretable: it survives *despite* the shrinking rank fraction, which strengthens it. **Outcome (B) is uninterpretable as written** — decay would be consistent with scale-dependent geometry and equally consistent with rank starvation, and the design cannot separate them. That is a pre-registration defect of the same class as the H2 triviality gap: an outcome that would read as a finding but measures something else.

**Required before H2-S locks, one of two options.** Either (i) add a **rank-fraction control arm**: at the 7B family, train a second small set at rank scaled to match the anchor's r/hidden (r ~ 56 at hidden 3584, which recovers 0.0156), so a decay result can be tested against the matched-fraction arm; or (ii) **rescope H2-S** to a fixed-rank claim, stating explicitly that it tests transfer of a fixed-capacity adapter across scale, not of task geometry across scale, and drop the "scale-local universality" reading from outcome (B). I prefer (i) — it costs one small arm and buys an interpretable negative — but (ii) is honest and free. **Do not run H2-S under the current wording.** H2-D is unaffected: Gemma-2-2B vs Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen-7B vs Llama-3.1-8B are near-matched in width, so lineage distance is not confounded with rank fraction the same way. Note in passing that E-T4 sits at rank 32 on hidden 4096, so r/h = **0.0078** — thinner than the anchor pair but *thicker* than the Qwen2.5-7B program family at 0.0067, so it is not the thinnest fraction in the program (the S1 alternate Mistral-7B at rank 24 would be, at 0.0059). Corrected 2026-08-04: I first reported 0.0059 for E-T4 by reusing the fixed-rank-24 column for a rank-32 model, and the "thinnest anywhere" reading built on it does not hold. Its canonical separation at 1M tokens stands on its own terms; it does not get extra credit from rank starvation.

## Part 4: A free spectral result

While sizing the confound I ran an ablation on the verified H2 feature matrices, no new compute. The spectrum representation is 384 dims = 4 depth bins x 4 projection types x 24 singular-value slots; truncating the slot axis and re-running standardized transfer gives a result I did not expect, and it survives a dimension-matched control.

| slots kept | dims | head (top-k) q->l / l->q | tail (bottom-k) q->l / l->q |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 16 | 0.3583 / 0.4500 | **0.9375 / 0.8833** |
| 2 | 32 | 0.5083 / 0.4833 | 0.9333 / 0.9208 |
| 4 | 64 | 0.6125 / 0.5417 | 0.8833 / 0.9875 |
| 8 | 128 | 0.5792 / 0.6583 | 0.7708 / 0.9708 |
| 16 | 256 | 0.6875 / 0.6667 | 0.6750 / 0.7875 |
| 24 (full) | 384 | 0.7833 / 0.7375 | 0.7833 / 0.7375 |

**Cross-family task structure lives in the smallest singular directions, not the largest.** The single smallest slot per block, sixteen dimensions total, transfers at **0.9375 / 0.8833** — better than the full 384-dimensional spectrum that carries the reported headline. The head does the opposite: the largest slot alone transfers at 0.3583 / 0.4500, and adding tail slots is what lifts the head curve toward the band. Along the tail curve, *adding* head slots degrades transfer monotonically.

**The control that makes this more than a dimension count.** Sixteen dimensions could simply be a lucky subspace, so I built a dimension-matched null: 200 random 16-dimensional subsets of the same 384 features, same standardization, same classifier, scored as the mean of both directions. The null has mean 0.7540 (sd 0.0764, 99th percentile 0.9042). Against it, tail-1 at **0.9104 exceeds 99.0% of random draws (empirical p = 0.010)**, and head-1 at 0.4042 exceeds **none** of them — it is the worst 16-dimensional choice available, not merely a weak one. The most uncomfortable number for the paper's framing: **the full 384-dimensional spectrum, at 0.7604, is beaten by 46.5% of random 16-dimensional subsets.** The pinned primary representation is, on this pair, no better than a random slice of itself.

Three further checks before I would say any of this out loud. Relative dispersion is roughly constant head to tail (sd/mean ~0.5-0.7 at slots 0 and 23), so per-feature standardization equalizes slot contributions rather than amplifying a noise floor. Unstandardized transfer sits at exactly 0.1667 for every truncation, head and tail alike, confirming the family shift dominates without the control. And the family-identity probe on tail-1 standardized features reads 0.4729 against chance 0.5, so the triviality control still holds where the effect is strongest.

**One caveat that bounds the reading.** Under the pinned H2 margin rule, tail-1's within-minus-cross margins are 6.2pp (qwen->llama) and 11.7pp (llama->qwen), both below the 15pp threshold — so on the tail representation the *margin* condition for "transfer fails" is not met either, which is consistent with transfer succeeding, but it also means within-family accuracy is at ceiling (1.0000 both families) and the margin term carries no discriminating information there. The margin rule was designed for a representation where within-family accuracy is informative; at 16 dimensions it is saturated.

**Disposition: exploratory, quarantined, and pre-registered before it becomes a claim.** It was computed after seeing this pair's H2 results, which is exactly the position I put H2-V in when I demoted it from co-primary, and the same rule binds me. **Do not put it in the Asset-1 condensation as a finding.** Register it on the H2-at-scale card, where the new families are unblinded, as a pre-declared head-versus-tail contrast with both outcomes stated: either the tail advantage replicates across new lineages and scales, in which case "task identity is stored in the small singular directions of the update" is a substantive and attackable claim about LoRA structure, or it is specific to this pair and demoted honestly.

Two reasons it matters beyond its own interest. It interacts directly with §3: if transfer is carried by the smallest singular directions, then holding rank fixed while width grows removes precisely the slots that carry it, which converts my rank-fraction objection from a design nicety into a prediction — decay under fixed rank should be steeper than decay under matched rank fraction, and control (i) would measure exactly that. And it bears on the D3 interpretive caveat already in the paper: a merge-conflict signal that lives in small singular directions is a different mechanism story than one living in the dominant update geometry.

## Part 5: Dataset method, three asks

**Ask 1: attack the governing claim.** I accept the claim's core and reject one load-bearing piece of it.

Accepted: corrections carry more information per record than accepted outputs, and the `accept`-versus-silence split is the right insight, correctly identified as a third of the signal being invisible. Making an unchanged answer emit a record is the same move as the family-identity probe — instrumenting the case where "nothing happened" and "we did not look" were indistinguishable. The `adapt`/`redo` split is equally sound: conflating substitution with error would train the model against its own correct answers, and that error compounds rather than averaging out.

**Rejected: "not in the volume of examples" is doing more work than the evidence supports, and it is the sentence most likely to hurt you.** Correction density is a property of the *capture instrument*; the sample size needed to fit an adapter is a property of the *estimation problem*. A corpus can be maximally informative per record and still be too small to fit anything, and the two claims are independent. At N=23 with 18 corrections spread over 8 kinds, several kinds have 1-2 instances; `redo` and `parse` have one each. Nothing about rank, LR, or step count is identifiable from that. The defensible version, which I would hold you to, is: *a correction-instrumented record yields more signal per example, so the volume required is lower than an uninstrumented corpus would need — not that volume is irrelevant.* You would learn the difference at N=23 only if you stated a target N and a per-kind floor and then measured against it, which you have not. **I would want a stated minimum per correction kind before any bank, not a total.**

Two further notes. Your §8 rejection of the rank-8-16 critique is right on the reasoning and slightly wrong in how it is framed: the critique's error is not that it used scaling-law logic, it is that example count is the wrong predictor for rank when the target is a *behavior* rather than *coverage of a label space*. Rank tracks the dimensionality of the update you need, not the number of examples you have; that is why style LoRAs want higher rank than likeness LoRAs at similar dataset sizes. State it that way and the critique is answered on mechanism rather than authority. And the `assumptions` field as reasoning channel is the correct workaround for an encrypted chain of thought, but be aware you are training on *post-hoc stated* reasoning, which is a different distribution from the reasoning that actually produced the answer. Flag it in every artifact as you already do in the report.

**Ask 2: yes, §6 needs a pre-registration, and your reading of the precedent is correct.** The regularization class has a comparison built into it (with and without the recovery projection), which makes it a hypothesis and not a design decision. Running it after seeing the corpus is precisely the forking path the discipline exists to close. Requirements, minimal but not optional: the comparison arms and the primary endpoint declared before any bank; a golden eval set existing *first*, since §7 already concedes there is no way to judge a trained artifact without one and I would not accept a claim from you without it either; a stated N and per-kind floor per Ask 1; and the DreamBooth analogy tested rather than assumed — prior preservation trades subject fidelity for generality, so the pre-registration must state what regression on the target task would count as too much. Note the analogy is imperfect in a way worth writing down: DreamBooth's prior class is sampled from the *base model's own* distribution, whereas your recovery examples are constructed from corrections, so they are not a prior in the same sense. That difference is interesting rather than fatal, but it should be named.

**Ask 3: what would make the `measured` anchor convincing.** It currently rests on argument, and the fix is cheap. Three things, in order of value. First, **a deliberate calibration set**: weigh and log 20-30 items spanning the range, before any training, so anchored records exist by design rather than by luck; without this the anchored subset is whatever happened to be on a scale, which is not a sample of anything. Second, **a measured false-positive rate on the flag**: take the records where `measured = true` and check by hand what fraction genuinely had an amount from a measurement rather than a label read; the prompt instructs FALSE-when-unsure, and whether the model complies is an empirical question you can answer today at N=23. Third, **the estimate-versus-anchor gap as a reported number**: on anchored records, the model's pre-correction amount against the weighed amount gives you a bias and a spread in grams, which is the only thing that turns "as accurate as the PI's eye" from a caveat into a measured quantity. That third item is what would convince me, because it is the same measurement-versus-projection distinction you correctly drew in §4, closed with data instead of stated as an intention.
